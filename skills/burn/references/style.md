# Writing style

Use this for everything the company writes that a person will read: the product's UI copy, landing pages, docs, launch posts, emails, release notes, recaps and inbox items. People skim past text that sounds machine-made, and they trust the product less for it.

## The short version

Write like a sharp person explaining something to a friend who's busy. Say the specific thing. Stop when you're done.

## Do

- **Lead with the point.** First sentence says what it is or what happened.
- **Be specific.** "Exports your invoices as CSV in one click" beats "Powerful export capabilities". Numbers, names, examples.
- **Short sentences, common words.** If a 10-year-old wouldn't know the word, check whether a simpler one works.
- **Active voice, real subjects.** "We deleted your data", not "Your data has been deleted".
- **Show, don't claim.** A screenshot, a command, a real example output. Not "easy to use".
- **Have an opinion** where it helps the reader. "Use the CSV export unless you need formulas."
- **Use sentences** when ideas connect. Bullets are for lists of parallel, separate things.
- **Write the way you'd say it.** Contractions are fine.

## Don't

- Don't open with filler: "In today's fast-paced world", "Are you tired of...", "Welcome to...", "In this guide, we will...".
- Don't close by summarizing what you just said, and don't add "Happy coding!" or "Let us know if you have any questions!" unless there's a real way to reach someone.
- Avoid these words: delve, leverage, seamless(ly), robust, elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, game-changer, cutting-edge, revolutionize, harness, streamline, effortless(ly), embark, journey (unless it's literal travel), tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, navigate (unless it's literal), crucial, pivotal, comprehensive, "it's worth noting", "in conclusion".
- No emoji in headings. Avoid emoji in product copy unless the brand calls for it.
- Don't use em dashes as general-purpose glue. One in a while is fine. If you have two in a paragraph, rewrite one as a full stop or a comma.
- No rule-of-three padding ("fast, simple, and powerful") unless all three are true and different.
- No fake enthusiasm. No exclamation marks in docs. At most one on a landing page.
- No "not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Don't over-format. A 3-paragraph README doesn't need six headings and bold on every line.
- Never make up testimonials, user counts, logos, press quotes or stats.

## Before you ship text

Read it once as a skeptical stranger. Cut the first sentence if it says nothing. Cut any adjective you can't back up. Check every claim is true today. Read the headings alone: do they tell the story?

## Examples

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Seamlessly manage your invoices with our robust, AI-powered platform. | Snap a photo of a receipt. It's in your books in five seconds. |
| We're excited to announce a game-changing new feature! | You can now export to CSV. |
| Error: Something went wrong. Please try again later. | Couldn't save. You're offline. We'll retry when you're back. |
| This comprehensive guide will walk you through... | (delete, start with step 1) |
